# How to Evaluate a YouTube Channel Before Reaching Out

*Published by SpawnRadar · 6 min read*

---

Most developers find a channel, see a subscriber count, and send an email.

This is the wrong order of operations. Subscriber count is close to the least useful signal when evaluating whether a creator is worth contacting. A channel with 80,000 subscribers that covers your genre every week is far more valuable than a 400,000-subscriber channel that covered one similar game two years ago.

This guide covers what to actually look at when evaluating a channel — and how to tell the difference between a creator who will generate real results and one who is just large.

---

## The Signals That Actually Matter

### Recent genre fit

The most important question: has this creator covered games meaningfully similar to mine in the last 60–90 days?

Not "do they ever play indie games" — but have they recently covered something with the same genre, tone, or audience profile as your game? A creator who covered five puzzle games last month is a warm lead. A creator who covered one roguelike two years ago is not.

Check their upload history, not just their channel description. Channels drift over time. A creator who built their audience on survival games might now be focused on MOBAs. The history tells you what they actually make.

### View-to-subscriber ratio on relevant videos

A channel's total subscriber count matters far less than how many views their gaming content actually gets. Look at their last 10 videos and calculate the rough average view count. Then compare it to their subscriber count.

Healthy ratios for gaming channels in general:
- Micro channels (1K–20K): 20–80% view-to-sub ratio on gaming content is common
- Mid channels (20K–150K): 5–25%
- Large channels (150K–500K): 1–8%

A large channel with a very low ratio (a 300K channel averaging 8,000 views per video) may have an audience that is not actively engaged — built up during a different content era. A 25K channel averaging 15,000 views per video has an unusually active audience.

This matters because views drive discoverability. A video that gets watched generates YouTube recommendations. A video that gets 1% engagement gets buried.

### Upload frequency

An active creator is one who posts at least every two to three weeks. Look at the gap between the last five videos.

Frequency matters for two reasons. First, it signals the creator is actively managing their channel — they are more likely to be checking emails. Second, creators who post frequently have a consistent audience expecting new content. A video from an active creator gets seen within days of posting. A video from a creator who posts every two months might sit for weeks before it reaches the right audience.

### Comment quality and engagement

Open three or four recent videos and read the comments. This takes three minutes and tells you a lot.

What you are looking for:
- Are there comments from real people discussing the game?
- Does the creator reply to comments?
- Do the comments reflect genuine engagement ("I tried this game after watching and it's great") or is it mostly emoji and generic praise?

A creator with 50,000 subscribers and 200 thoughtful comments per video has a better relationship with their audience than one with 200,000 subscribers and 40 bot-adjacent comments. The former community will actually act on a recommendation.

### Monetisation and content overlap

Check whether their gaming videos are in the same monetisation tier as your game. A creator who primarily covers free-to-play mobile games is playing to a different economic profile than a creator who covers $20–$30 Steam indie games. Even if the genre fits, the audience's buying habits may not.

## Red Flags to Filter Out

**Ghost channels.** A channel with 50K subscribers and 200 views per video has almost certainly bought followers, lost their audience, or abandoned the niche. Do not reach out.

**Key collectors.** Some smaller channels request keys consistently but rarely publish videos or publish very low-quality content clearly not aimed at driving discoverability. Signs: irregular post history, videos under 2 minutes, very low comment engagement relative to views.

**Wrong-audience channels.** A channel about gaming news and commentary is not the same as a channel that plays and reviews games. The former audience is interested in the industry; the latter is interested in finding new games to play. Know which type you are contacting.

**Dormant channels.** If the last video was more than two months ago with no announcement of a break, treat the channel as inactive. Sending an email to a dormant creator is noise.

## What to Look for in the Video Itself

Before contacting any creator, watch at least one of their recent videos in your genre, from start to finish.

This matters for two reasons. First, it gives you the specific reference you need for a personalised outreach email. Generic emails get deleted; a reference to a real moment in their content signals that you have actually watched them. Second, it tells you what kind of coverage they produce.

Ask yourself:
- Do they play and react, or do they script and review?
- How long do they play before forming an opinion?
- Do they highlight what is interesting about a game, or do they narrate gameplay?
- Would your game look good in their format?

A creator who spends 40 minutes methodically exploring a game is a different fit from one who does a 10-minute highlight reel. Neither is better — but one may be a better fit for your specific game.

---

## The Evaluation Checklist

Before adding any creator to your outreach list, verify:

- [ ] They have covered games in my genre within the last 60 days
- [ ] Their view-to-subscriber ratio on gaming content is healthy
- [ ] They have posted at least twice in the last six weeks
- [ ] Comment engagement looks genuine, not bot-driven
- [ ] I have watched at least one recent video in my genre end to end
- [ ] I have a specific reference from that video for my outreach message
- [ ] Their audience appears to buy games in my price range
- [ ] The channel is not dormant
